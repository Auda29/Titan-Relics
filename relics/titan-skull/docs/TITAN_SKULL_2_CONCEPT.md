## Observed anatomy and defining feature

- A broad, rounded, roughly humanoid skull faces the viewer in a damp cavern.
  Two large dark sockets, a triangular nasal opening and a wide open mouth are
  readable despite heavy moss and hanging vegetation.
- The lower jaw and rows of substantial teeth are visible; water/mist and growth
  obscure the lowest ground contact. The mouth is a deep void, not a painted mark.
- **A huge sword is embedded diagonally in the upper-left part of the cranium
  as seen in the image.** Its broad blade, crossguard and wrapped-looking grip
  are distinct. It leans roughly 15–20 degrees left from vertical in projection.
- The crossguard spans roughly half the skull's projected width. This is a rough
  image relationship, not a same-depth engineering measurement.
- The cranium is rounded rather than crowned. No organic crown, long horns,
  spine or ribs are demonstrated in the selected shot.
- Stalactites, stalagmites, shafts of light and wet ground frame the scene. These
  are environmental elements, not bony projections attached to the skull.
- The skull's rear, hidden sword tip, precise penetration path and grip/pommel
  termination are not fully visible and must be authored later.

## Design identity

This is a **second independent Titan Skull design**, not V5 or a replacement for
the existing crowned skull. The current skull's V1–V4 development and four V4
ground palettes remain separate and unchanged.

The new design should broaden the Titan-remains family with a recognizably
different silhouette and anatomy, rather than only recoloring the first skull.
Specific features must follow an actual visual review of the selected segment.

The following values are this study's recommended working specification,
not yet a user-approved model. See the [shared four-relic study](../../../docs/project/NEXT_FOUR_RELIC_DESIGN_STUDY.md).

## Working dimensions and proportions

| Element | Proposed dimensions / placement |
| --- | --- |
| Whole skull/sword/local-deposit envelope | **104 W × 144 H × 116 D** |
| Nominal burial allowance | **12** below ground |
| Total visible height including sword | **132** |
| Skull alone | **82 W × 76 H × 90 D**, about **64** above ground |
| Eye openings | Each about **22 W × 18 H**, recessed **16–20** |
| Nose opening | Approximately **11 W × 15 H** |
| Mouth opening | Approximately **31 W × 21 H**, **25–30** deep |
| Main bone shell | **5–8** thick; heavier **8–12** jaw/cheek supports |
| Sword above cranium | Approximately **72** along its axis, raising silhouette about **68** |
| Exposed blade / grip and pommel | Approximately **44 / 28** along the axis |
| Blade width/thickness | **10–12 / 4–6** |
| Crossguard span | Approximately **38–42**, around half skull width |
| Sword lean | **18°** toward image-left in the main view; minor **5–8°** rearward lean |

The bone silhouette stays near the existing Titan/large-Colossus-head scale;
the weapon provides the extra height. The skull width/visible bone height is
about **1.28**. Keep large socket and mouth openings readable under vegetation.
The sword tip continues invisibly into the skull/substrate for roughly **18–24**
blocks as an authored completion, rather than floating on top of the forehead.

## Destruction, burial and material direction

**Localized violent impact plus long erosion:** target **12–18% missing
reconstructed bone volume**, excluding natural sockets, mouth and cranial cavity.
The exact percentage is a future authoring target, not measured reference loss.

- One concentrated **14–18**-wide impact opening around the blade, with two
  short radiating fractures. Preserve the broad cranial dome and both cheeks.
- Two missing/broken upper teeth and one lower tooth, with unequal remaining
  teeth. Avoid a regular smiling tooth grid or taking away the entire jaw.
- Keep two jaw/cheek load paths and a stable sword/cranial contact. The rear
  remains a complete weathered shell with one modest secondary erosion opening.
- Place the lower jaw **8–12** into irregular damp deposits while retaining
  an open mouth threshold. Do not bury the face to simulate destruction.
- Moss/roots may occupy roughly **35–45% of upward-facing exterior surfaces**;
  preserve bone borders around sockets, the nose and enough of the crossguard
  to identify the weapon. Growth is not missing bone.
- Do not add the first skull's nine-vertebra spine or crown without a separate
  design decision. A few local chips are enough for this compact composition.

Working environment: **lush grotto or a broad, partly open sinkhole**, using
existing terrain for the enclosing walls and daylight opening. This adapts the
reference cavern without exporting an entire cave roof. A fully underground
placement would need a deliberately verified cavern with enough clearance for
the 132-block above-ground composition; ordinary cave fit is not assumed.

Palette direction: weathered pale bone/limestone, damp gray-brown deposits,
muted moss and a dark mineralized/rusted-looking sword. Large weapon surfaces
should use low-value materials rather than solid iron/diamond blocks. The exact
palette and any small salvage quantities are a later export study, not gameplay
loot decisions. No glowing blade, usable giant weapon item or named owner is
inferred from the reference.

## Lore and gameplay

**Approved October 8 history:** the central large Colossus and smaller crewed
companions conducted the Titan-clearing campaign thousands of years ago. The
Titans worshipped the World Tree and received strength and size from it. Their
exact nature and the campaign's motive are lost to time. **No living Titans remain**;
ordinary current mobs and players reuse the remains. Obelisks marked cleared
territories, with skulls scattered widely across their surroundings. Exact regional
placement is specified in the [shared distribution contract](../../../docs/technical/RELIC_DISTRIBUTION.md),
with real density still untested. This does not decide the sword's owner or add a boss.

The new skull belongs to the same family-level discovery budget as the existing
skull and future bone variants. The updated relatively most frequent tier contains
both skull designs and Obelisks, followed by rarer Tower/small Colossi/Tree, then
the unique large Colossus. All remain genuinely rare; rates/distances are approved
starting values with real density still untested. Adding the design must not multiply total
Titan-remains family density unintentionally. Large-site uniqueness is not yet
implemented.

Use the existing [skull gameplay direction](../gameplay/README.md): ordinary mobs
inhabit the decaying remains; no exclusive item, trial, or custom enemy is
required. Current interiors/access and natural spawning are retained; no additional
encounters, access work or loot chests are selected for either skull design.

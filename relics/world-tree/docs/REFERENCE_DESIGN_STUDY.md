## Observed in the reference

[The Archive In Between, reel DaBgsX0C9s4](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DaBgsX0C9s4/).
Inspected 0–48 s every three seconds and 51–105 s every six seconds.

- Opening/coastal scenes show a colossal cypress-like tree: an irregular,
  strongly fluted or twisted trunk and broad horizontal foliage layers.
- Large branches remain readable beneath foliage. The crown is divided into
  offset masses; it is neither a spherical leaf ball nor a uniform cone.
- At 6 s, small buildings are lodged beside trunk/root forms. At 18–24 s,
  worked/carved stones sit among roots; 21 s clearly shows a large spiral relief.
- Around 30–36 s, tall root-side spaces, stone steps and passages emphasize
  human-scale access. Around 39–45 s, water winds among large root formations.
- The 48–57 s wide views are useful for whole-tree proportions; later views
  reinforce crown layering and a massive trunk, but do not form consistent
  orthographic views of one specimen.
- No single mandatory broken trunk or dying crown is established. The dominant
  impression is living growth around much older-looking structures.

Use **five major branching directions** carrying **6–8 unequal foliage masses**.
The broad upper crown occupies roughly Y120–208 above ground; two lower side
masses at Y86–138 create the layered profile and break symmetry. Do not place
equal leaf ellipsoids around a straight trunk.

## Damage and age

**Living, lightly damaged tree:** a single old missing major side branch below
the main crown, plus two or three smaller dead stubs. Keep loss below **5% of
major woody volume** relative to the reconstructed intact trunk/primary branches.
Natural branch spacing and leaf gaps do not count as destruction.

- Concentrate age in twisted bark, root buttresses, a healed branch scar and
  small sheltered hollows. Preserve a continuous believable trunk load path.
- Crown holes reveal branches, not disconnected floating leaf islands.
- Keep the main crown healthy and asymmetrical; no large saw-cut trunk opening
  or half-dead silhouette merely to make it look like a ruin.
- Reserve two or three worked-stone fragments **3–7** blocks high at the base.
  Their broken edges and partial engulfment carry the architectural decay.
- A root passage can re-emerge at another side; detailed paths, stairs and
  inhabited tree buildings are deferred rather than inferred from every scene.

## Landscape and material direction

Primary setting: a low green coast or broad river valley. Roots should contact
existing soil at several elevations; local deposits must not become a flat giant
disc. A side stream is optional and does not require an exported coastline.

The proposed height fits a standard Y319 ceiling at low terrain elevations;
with a 16-block margin, conservatively require nominal ground at or below Y95.
This is a future placement check, not an implementation in this study.

Use warm dark wood, muted deep green foliage and small moss/stone accents. The
future block palette must address leaf persistence and the quantity of harvestable
wood; exact states and resource quantities are not established by these pictures.

## Authored completion

Root layout on hidden sides, one selected crown composition, branch scar position,
absolute scale and the small stone fragments are new design decisions. The reel
is a montage, so architectural scenes are references for spatial relationships,
not a blueprint for a continuous tree city. First modeling review should show
the crown silhouette from four sides and a leaf-hidden branch inspection.
